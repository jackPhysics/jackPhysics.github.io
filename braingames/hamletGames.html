HAMLET:

Heard of 'Othello', the commercial name for Reversi?
Well 'Hamlet' is a puzzle game of my own invention based on Reversi.
The aim is to change the whole board into a single colour - should you change 2B? or not? That's the question... it's so hard to make the decision...
You can try with 2 to 5 colours, vary the tile flip rules, and most crucially, vary the 'colour choose' rules - that has the biggest effect on game play I think.
Does it work as a puzzle? Is it too easy or too hard? Hamlet himself would not be able to decide.

RULES: The aim is to make the whole board a single colour.
Clicking a square changes it to the current colour (shown at the top left).
You cannot change a square to the colour it already is and you cannot change a square two turns consecutively.
When you change a square to a new colour any squares trapped between it and any other squares of the same colour will be flipped to that colour.
If you are using more than 2 colours then all the intermediate, trapped, squares need to be of the exact same colour in order to be flipped.

'Hamlet' - random colours

The colours are chosen at semi-random, there is some attempt to avoid similar colours, but sometimes this does not work, particularly with shades of white - which all tend to look 'white'...
So, everytime you start a game, check the range of colours chosen at the top of the page to ensure you can tell the difference - if not refresh and start again - unless you want that extra challenge!
Me, personally, I like the excitement of different combinations of random colours - but it might not be your thing so study the other options below...

'Hamlet' - controlled random colours

The colours are chose at random, but the algorithm should ensure they are quite different to each other.
But, everytime you start a game, check the range of colours chosen at the top of the page to ensure you can tell the difference - if not refresh and start again - unless you want that extra challenge!

'Hamlet' - fixed colours

The colours are fixed - the same every time (but they do change depending on how many colours you choose for the gane).

'Hamlet' - semi-fixed colours

The colours are fixed, to make sure they are different, but with about 2 or 3 choices each.
